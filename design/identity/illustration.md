# Illustration Direction

> **Purpose:** Decide when illustration helps and define the visual language it must follow.
>
> **Pravaah OS:** v1.0.0  
> **Last Updated:** 2026-08-08  
> **Owner:** Founders  
> **Related Documents:** [Icon System](icon-system.md), [Texture Language](texture-language.md), [Photography Direction](photography.md)  
> **Estimated Reading Time:** 5 minutes

Illustration is optional. Pravaah should not use it to make an ordinary statement feel more important. It is appropriate only when it clarifies a system, transition, relationship, or idea that would otherwise require excessive text.

## Recommended language

Use abstract editorial compositions built from line, field, interval, and carefully bounded geometry. Think of ink meeting paper, a measured current moving through space, or two paths joining without becoming an obvious literal wave. Forms are simple, spacious, and quiet.

## Geometry

Use circles, arcs, straight lines, grids, and open shapes with visible construction logic. The flow line may bend gently but never becomes a liquid logo or ornamental ribbon. Forms must have a readable hierarchy at small size.

## Colour and material

Start in charcoal and paper. Moss supports information or direction; copper marks a rare point of energy. A static paper grain is permitted beneath an illustration only if it does not reduce contrast or readability. No gradient mesh, glass effect, glow, 3D object, fantasy landscape, cartoon character, or generic AI motif.

## Text equivalent

Every meaningful illustration has adjacent explanatory text. Decorative illustration is hidden from assistive technology. If an image cannot explain itself through a short sentence, reconsider whether it is useful.
