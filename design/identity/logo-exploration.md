# Logo Exploration

> **Purpose:** Explore five viable identity directions before committing to final logo artwork.
>
> **Pravaah OS:** v1.0.0  
> **Last Updated:** 2026-08-08  
> **Owner:** Founders  
> **Related Documents:** [Wordmark System](wordmark-system.md), [Decision Matrix](decision-matrix.md), [Rejected Ideas](../../decisions/rejected-ideas.md)  
> **Estimated Reading Time:** 9 minutes

These are strategic directions, not artwork instructions. No direction is a logo until it has been explored at real sizes, tested in monochrome, and approved by the founders.

## Direction 01 — The Measured Wordmark

**Idea:** `Pravaah` is the identity. A custom-drawn serif wordmark carries subtle editorial character, while a fine horizontal flow line is a separate supporting element.

**Why it could work:** It puts language and confidence before symbolism. It is legible in a browser tab, proposal cover, invoice header, and future software navigation. It can age gracefully because it relies on proportion, not a visual trick.

**Risk:** It requires excellent typography and spacing. A generic type treatment would feel unfinished.

## Direction 02 — The Flow Monogram

**Idea:** A single `P` is shaped by a continuous line or enclosed counter that suggests movement.

**Why it could work:** It creates a compact mark for favicons, app icons, and small applications while keeping a link to the name.

**Risk:** A forced flowing `P` can become a familiar technology mark or a decorative gesture. It must be rejected if legibility is sacrificed for metaphor.

## Direction 03 — The Confluence Mark

**Idea:** Two measured lines join and continue as one. The mark represents strategy and technology becoming sustained momentum.

**Why it could work:** It reflects the two-founder, two-discipline structure without using literal initials or a generic infinity loop.

**Risk:** It may be too abstract for a pre-launch company and can look like a consulting or financial-services symbol if over-polished.

## Direction 04 — The Editorial Seal

**Idea:** A small typographic seal or monogram accompanies a restrained wordmark, inspired by the ownership mark in a well-made book.

**Why it could work:** It lends gravity to proposals, invoices, notebook covers, and print applications while keeping the wordmark primary.

**Risk:** It can become nostalgic, overly formal, or falsely historic. Pravaah must not imply a heritage it has not earned.

## Direction 05 — The Horizon Line

**Idea:** A wordmark is paired with a single measured horizon or current line that changes position across applications but retains the same proportion and weight.

**Why it could work:** It gives the identity a living but restrained expression of flow. It works as composition rather than a decorative logo symbol.

**Risk:** If the line is treated as animation or ornament, it will weaken the identity. It needs strict rules and should never replace the name.

## Exploration rules

Each direction must be tested in black, white, and one accent colour; at 16px, 24px, 40px, and 160px; in navigation, favicon, social card, proposal cover, invoice, and signage simulation. No direction is advanced because it looks attractive in one oversized presentation.
