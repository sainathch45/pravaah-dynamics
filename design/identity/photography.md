# Photography Direction

> **Purpose:** Define how photographic work can express Pravaah with honesty and material calm.
>
> **Pravaah OS:** v1.0.0  
> **Last Updated:** 2026-08-08  
> **Owner:** Founders  
> **Related Documents:** [Imagery Direction](../../website/design/imagery-and-illustration.md), [Texture Language](texture-language.md), [Brand Applications](applications.md)  
> **Estimated Reading Time:** 6 minutes

Photography is evidence of attention, not a shortcut to looking established. Use it when it gives a viewer real context, scale, texture, or human presence that words cannot provide.

## Mood

Calm, observant, architectural, and human. Images feel discovered rather than staged. The subject has room to breathe, and the frame is confident enough to leave things out.

## Composition

Use asymmetry, clear planes, quiet negative space, and a single point of attention. Crop for the content area, not for maximum visual density. A photograph may support a headline, but text is never placed where contrast or subject integrity is compromised.

## Light and material

Prefer soft daylight, directional side light, gentle shadow, and natural colour. Useful materials include paper, stone, brushed metal, wood, concrete, ink, glass used sparingly, and real working surfaces. Retouching corrects distraction but does not invent an environment or outcome.

## People

Show real people in real contexts only with permission and a clear reason. Favour concentration, collaboration, making, and thoughtful pause over staged celebration. Represent people with dignity; do not use a person as a generic signal for innovation or diversity.

## Never use

Stock-office theatre, posed handshakes, headset smiles, generic city skylines, robot imagery, floating device mockups, extreme colour grading, neon light, fake client environments, or scenes that imply a team, office, project, or capability that Pravaah has not earned.

## Treatment

Use charcoal, paper, moss, and copper as grading references, not overlays. Avoid obvious filters. Images may be monochrome only when the loss of colour strengthens the composition and accessibility remains intact.
